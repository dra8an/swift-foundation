# Umm al-Qura (Saudi Arabia)

> Research notes for the Swift Islamic calendar rules. Every statement says whether its source is **official** (the authority's own site or document), **other** (a secondary source) or **none** (not found). Collected 2026-11-05 by web research that identified itself honestly and respected robots.txt; no data was taken from ICOP (astronomycenter.net) or moonsighting.com.

Umm al-Qura calendar, Saudi Arabia. It is the official civil and administrative Hijri calendar, computed by a committee chaired by the president of KACST (King Abdulaziz City for Science and Technology). The religious months are decided by sighting, through the Supreme Court's crescent circuit (دائرة الأهلة في المحكمة العليا).

## Criterion

**In use today** (official): Official printed calendar, 'Taqwim Umm al-Qura al-Muqaran 1420-1450 AH / 1999-2029 CE / 1377-1407 SH', Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, Ministry of Finance and National Economy. The preface page is signed by Dr. Saleh bin Abdulrahman Al-Adhel, 'رئيس لجنة اعداد ومراجعة تقويم أم القرى، رئيس مدينة الملك عبدالعزيز للعلوم والتقنية' (Chair of the Committee for Preparing and Reviewing the Umm al-Qura Calendar; President of KACST), PDF page 9. Verbatim: 'أولاً: أسس إعداد حسابات التقويم: 1- ألا يزيد عدد أيام الشهر الهجري على 30 يوما. 2- ألا ينقص عدد أيام الشهر القمري عن 29 يوما. 3- عدد أشهر العام الهجري القمري اثنا عشر شهرا. ثانياً: اعتبارات حسابات التقويم: اعتمد معدو التقويم الهجري القمري على اعتبار غروب الشمس من مكة المكرمة قبل القمر بداية للشهر، وذلك تنفيذاً لما جاء في لائحة تحري رؤية هلال أوائل الشهور القمرية (المادة الحادية عشرة) الصادرة بقرار مجلس الوزراء رقم 143 في 1418/8/22هـ. ثالثاً: شروط بداية الشهر الهجري القمري: 1- أن يكون كامل جرم القمر (الهلال) فوق الأفق بعد غروب الشمس. 2- أن يكون غروب القمر بعد غروب الشمس (في مكة المكرمة) بحيث يكون بالإمكان رؤيته وقد تخلق فيه النور. 3- ولادة الهلال فلكياً. رابعاً: إحداثيات نقطة ولادة الهلال فلكياً: ... خط الطول: 39 درجة و49 دقيقة و32 ثانية. خط العرض: 21 درجة و25 دقيقة و21 ثانية. الارتفاع: 295 متراً عن سطح البحر.' The same page also says: 'إذ تطرح هذا التقويم الممتد من 1420هـ إلى 1450هـ (أي مدة ثلاثين عاماً قمرياً) ... وتؤكد أن التقويم هو اصطلاحي مدني لأغراض الحساب فقط حيث إن العدة في مجال العبادات هي الرؤية الشرعية للهلال'. Current official website (ummulqura.org.sa, /assets/data/pages.json, about-calendar, lastUpdate 2026-04-23): 'يعتمد التقويم منذ عام 1420هـ على إحداثيات الكعبة المشرفة بمكة المكرمة أساسًا للحسابات الفلكية، ويتم تحديد بداية الشهر عند ولادة الهلال وغروب القمر بعد غروب الشمس في مكة المكرمة.' English version on the same site: 'Since 1420 AH, the coordinates of the Holy Kaaba in Makkah have been used as the basis for astronomical calculations. A new month begins when the crescent is born and the moon sets after sunset in Makkah.'

*English:* Calculation basis: a month has at most 30 and at least 29 days, and a year has 12 months. Sunset at Makkah, occurring before moonset, is taken as the start of the month, in application of Article 11 of the Regulation on Sighting the Crescents of the Beginnings of Lunar Months (Council of Ministers resolution 143, 22/8/1418 AH = 1997-12-22). Conditions for a new month: (1) the whole body of the moon (the crescent) is above the horizon after sunset; (2) moonset is after sunset at Makkah, 'so that it is possible to see it and light has formed in it'; (3) the astronomical birth of the crescent (conjunction). Reference point: longitude 39°49'32" E, latitude 21°25'21" N, elevation 295 m. The printed tables cover AH 1420-1450 (30 lunar years). The calendar is a civil convention for reckoning only, and for worship the count follows the legal sighting of the crescent. The 2026 website text is shorter: the month begins when the crescent is born and the moon sets after sunset in Makkah, with Kaaba coordinates used since 1420 AH. Neither official text says on which evening the test is made (the 29th) or defines conditions (1) and (2) in computational terms, i.e. which limb, refraction, or topocentric vs geocentric. The official texts do not separate a 1420 rule from a 1423 rule. That split comes only from secondary sources.

**Since:** Official sources give AH 1420 (1999). The website says 'منذ عام 1420هـ' and the official printed table covers 1420-1450 with the three conditions above. Saudipedia (Ministry of Media) also gives 1420. Al-Watan (2015-10-14) says the two conditions (conjunction before sunset and sunset before moonset at Makkah) were 'applied in the Umm al-Qura calendar starting from 1420 AH'. The secondary source van Gent gives a different date: the two-condition rule from AH 1423 (2002-03-15), with moonset-only in 1420-1422, modified after anomalies in Rajab 1421 and Sha'ban 1422. Conflict: the official texts do not mention a 1423 change.

**Coverage.** Main results. (1) The official criterion text was found in the official printed calendar 'Taqwim Umm al-Qura al-Muqaran 1420-1450 AH' (Saudi Ministry of Finance and National Economy, computed by a KACST-chaired committee). I read a scan of it hosted by van Gent at https://webspace.science.uu.nl/~gent0113/islam/downloads/umm_al_qura_1420_1450.pdf; van Gent's bibliography dates it [2003]. It states: month length 29-30 days only; sunset at Makkah before moonset as the start of the month, citing Council of Ministers resolution 143 (1418) Art. 11; three conditions (whole lunar body above the horizon after sunset, moonset after sunset at Makkah, astronomical birth); and reference point 39°49'32"E, 21°25'21"N, 295 m. The 2026 official website (ummulqura.org.sa, KACST) gives a shorter version and dates it 'since 1420 AH'. The 1420 vs 1423 split exists only in secondary sources (van Gent). (2) I transcribed all 132 official month starts for AH 1440-1450 from that printed table (PDF pp.131-196, two months per page). Each weekday matched and each month length was 29 or 30 days. All 132 are identical to the ICU Umm al-Qura table as exposed by Apple Foundation on this machine (Calendar(identifier: .islamicUmmAlQura)). Court statements on uqn.gov.sa (1442-1447) and uqn.gov.sa page headers (1448 months 1-5) agree with the table. (3) The printed table explicitly ends at 1450. The older .NET limit (2029-05-13 = end of 1450) matches that horizon. The 2026 website offers years 1318-1500, and .NET 4.5+ goes to 1500 'licensed from the Saudi government'. ICU-10695 (2014) says KACST supplied 'official data from year 1300 to 1600'. So the post-1450 years in our table are claimed to be KACST data, not an ICU extrapolation. However, I found no official statement of how KACST computes them or whether they follow the 1423/1420 rule. A third-party library (calendrical) says KACST's tables from 1451 start about a third of months one day later than the rule and never earlier, calling them 'KACST's projections'. (4) Deviations: within 1440-1448, Dhu al-Qa'da 1447 (Supreme Court decision 206/H completed Shawwal to 30 days, 2026-04-19 instead of 2026-04-18) and Muharram 1443 (van Gent). The 16 older cases come from van Gent. (5) Terms of use: the official site states no licence for reuse. uqn.gov.sa prohibits copying and automated scraping. No source grants open-source redistribution of the dates. I did not access ICOP/astronomycenter.net or moonsighting.com.
